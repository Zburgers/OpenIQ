# Research Ethics and Governance

OpenIQ can be used privately for engineering/replication work without automatically becoming a human-subject research study. The moment we intentionally recruit people or collect data for a publishable/generalizable research question, governance changes.

## Before publication-directed participant recruitment

Obtain guidance from an appropriate institutional Ethics Committee/IRB/IEC regarding review, exemption, or other applicable pathway **before** collecting the research dataset.

Prepare at minimum:

- protocol and research questions
- participant information sheet
- informed-consent text/process
- eligibility/age policy
- recruitment materials
- compensation policy, if any
- risks/benefits and debriefing
- withdrawal process
- collected variables and data-flow diagram
- pseudonymization/access-control plan
- retention/deletion policy
- data-sharing/publication plan
- preregistration/statistical analysis plan where appropriate

## India references

Use the current ICMR guidance index (https://www.icmr.gov.in/guidelines), including the National Ethical Guidelines for Biomedical and Health Research Involving Human Participants and the ICMR Research Integrity and Publication Ethics policy, while seeking institution-specific advice on applicability.

## Current project constraint

At bootstrap, the only confirmed participant is the developer, with perhaps a few informal volunteers available. **That is useful for usability/debugging but not enough to establish population norms, robust IRT calibration, DIF, or general validity.** Do not convert a tiny convenience sample into an “IQ norm.”

## External expertise

Before strong claims/publication, seek review/collaboration from someone with demonstrated psychometrics/psychological measurement expertise. A general psychology background is useful but not a substitute for relevant measurement expertise.
